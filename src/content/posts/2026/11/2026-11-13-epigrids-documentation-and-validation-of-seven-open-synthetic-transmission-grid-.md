This paper documents and checks EPIGRIDS, seven open synthetic transmission grid cases from four independently built networks, made with an optimization-based construction method instead of the graph-statistics method behind most large public synthetic grids. The cases range from 250 to 78,484 buses, with geolocated substations, voltages up to 765 kV, and line parameters taken from conductor data. Each ships under an open license in three common power flow formats that agree at the solved operating point, and the six larger cases have a companion hourly, bus-level load series for one year. The Texas, Florida, and Michigan, Ohio, and Indiana cases are sub-networks of the Eastern case. Every case solves an alternating-current power flow from its stored operating point. With parallel circuits merged, the Eastern and Western cases are 9 and 16% denser than the real interconnections, while graph-statistics cases on the same footprints lie within 6% and meet more published realism criteria. A full single-outage contingency analysis completes on every case, and in an optimal power flow benchmark every case except the New England base case and one of its 12 operating points solves in polar form.
