The quality of a model resulting from (black-box) system identification is highly dependent on the quality of the data that is used during the identification procedure. As nonlinear system identification is sensitive to modeling and extrapolation errors, having experiments that explore the considered operation range of interest are of high importance. The Gaussian-Process based space-filling input design strategy introduces an experiment design technique that aims to achieve a space-filling distribution within the argument space of a (nonlinear) approximator used for modeling the true system. Experiments carried out on simulation examples show that models trained with space-filling signals generalize better over the region of interest compared to models obtained from non-space-filling experiments. Moreover, the method is connected to the optimality theory of experiment design (V- and D-optimal design), allowing the experimenter to tailor the designed space-filling signal to maximize its information content with respect to the experiment objective.
